<div class="research-project">
  <span class="research-tag">Palaeontology · Herpetology</span>
  <h2>Modern herpetofaunas and the fossil record</h2>
  <p>
    During my postgraduate training I have focused on the fossil record of tropical South American herpetofaunas, with a particular emphasis on snakes. I have described several fossils of the enigmatic Miocene snake <em>Colombophis</em> (<a href="https://doi.org/10.5252/geodiversitas2023v45a13" target="_blank">Alfonso-Rojas et al. 2023</a>) from the famous biota of La Venta (<a href="https://doi.org/10.5252/geodiversitas2023v45a26" target="_blank">Carrillo et al. 2023</a>), and traced back the evolutionary origin of gigantism in anacondas (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.2025.2572967" target="_blank">Alfonso-Rojas et al. 2025</a>). Additional ongoing work includes the description of a new lineage of Miocene snakes (in revision) and multiple fossil colubroids from the Neotropics.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="research-project">
  <span class="research-tag">Quaternary Palaeontology · Biogeography</span>
  <h2>Quaternary faunas of northern South America</h2>
  <p>
    In collaboration with archaeologists and palaeontologists, I have worked on describing the faunas that the first humans encountered upon arriving in South America. This includes the Late Pleistocene assemblage from the archaeological site of Pubenza, Colombia (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/jqs.3186" target="_blank">Alfonso-Rojas et al. 2021</a>) — which yielded turtles, mammals, birds, invertebrates, and plant remains — and ongoing work at the Taima-Taima Archaeological and Paleontological Park in Venezuela (in revision).
  </p>
</div>

      <span class="research-tag">Taphonomy · Molecular Palaeontology</span>
       <h2>Preservation of organic components in fossils</h2>
      <p>
      In collaboration with <a href="https://www.paleoturtles.com/" target="_blank">Dr Edwin Cadena</a> (Universidad del Rosario), I have explored the extraordinary preservation of organic compounds in Colombian fossil material. This has led to the discovery of exceptionally preserved 'skin' in an Early Cretaceous fish (<a href="https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.9479" target="_blank">Alfonso-Rojas & Cadena 2020</a>) and soft-tissue preservation in a Cretaceous ichthyosaur (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cretres.2026.106305" target="_blank">Martinez-Motta et al. 2026</a>), contributing to our understanding of preservation pathways in tropical depositional settings.</p>
